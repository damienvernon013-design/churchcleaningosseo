The Sunday Turnaround Cleaning Checklist

What needs to happen between services when you have 45 minutes and one custodian.

Two-service Sunday mornings are standard for growing congregations. The 9am service ends around 10:15, the 10:30 service starts at 10:30, and somewhere in that 15 minutes the sanctuary needs to be ready for a fresh group of 150 to 400 people. Add a 15-minute congregational fellowship break to 45 and you have the realistic window: 45 minutes, one person, a sanctuary that just held a full service.

This checklist is sequenced for efficiency. The order matters: you clear the biggest visible issues first, then work systematically so nothing is missed.

First 10 minutes: the entrance and narthex

The entrance is what the arriving congregation sees before they see anything else. Empty the waste bin at the entrance if it has significant content. Sweep or mat-shake the entry if weather has brought debris. Spot-wipe the glass doors if there are visible handprints from the exiting crowd. Stock the bulletin holder and attendance pad if those are positioned in the narthex.

Minutes 10 to 25: the sanctuary itself

Walk the pews or chairs and collect bulletins, tissue, forgotten items and any cups or wrappers from families with children. Do this in a single pass from front to back on one side, back to front on the other. This also serves as your visual inspection: you will notice the pew that was moved, the hymnal that got left open on the floor, the chair that is out of alignment.

Align chairs or straighten pews as you go. A sanctuary where the seating looks deliberate communicates something different than one where the rows look like people just got up and left.

If you have offering envelopes or communion supplies to reset, do this now before you add floor work.

Minutes 25 to 35: the restrooms

A full-service restroom clean is not realistic in this window, but a check-and-restock is. Empty the trash if it is more than half full. Restock paper towels and toilet paper. Wipe the counter if there is visible water. Flush and inspect. This pass takes 3 to 5 minutes per restroom.

Minutes 35 to 45: floors and final check

A quick floor pass with a dust mop or push broom on the main aisle and the entryway picks up the worst of the tracked debris and dropped bulletins. This is not a thorough floor clean, it is a visible-cleanliness pass. Save the thorough floor work for Monday.

Final walk: stand at the back of the sanctuary and look forward. Check that the platform is clear and orderly, that the seating is reasonably aligned, and that nothing is visually distracting. If the sanctuary looks intentionally prepared, the arriving congregation will receive it that way.

What goes on Monday

The thorough clean—all-aisle vacuuming or sweeping, floor mopping, restroom deep clean, pew rail and surface wiping, window ledge dusting—belongs on Monday when you have time to do it properly. Trying to do it in the Sunday morning window produces a rushed result on both ends.
