10 Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Church Cleaning Company

Choosing a cleaning company is a relationship your congregation will likely live with for a year or more.

Choosing a church cleaning company is a relationship your facilities committee will likely live with for a year or more. A few good questions during the evaluation process can save months of frustration.

1. How long have you been in business, and do you have references from similar congregations?
Longevity and relevant experience matter. A company with experience cleaning churches understands pew care, sanctuary acoustics during cleaning hours, and nursery standards in a way a purely commercial-office cleaner may not.

2. Are your employees background-checked?
Cleaning crews often work in a building with unsupervised access to nursery and children's ministry spaces. Background checks for all staff should be non-negotiable.

3. Are you bonded and insured?
Ask for a current certificate of insurance including general liability and workers' compensation. If something breaks or an employee is injured on church property, you need to know the congregation is protected.

4. What cleaning products do you use, especially in nursery and children's spaces?
Product choices matter for spaces with regular child contact. Ask specifically about their disinfectants and whether they use different products in nursery areas.

5. Who will actually clean our building?
Some companies send sales staff to quote, then hand off to subcontractors. Know who's actually doing the work.

6. What happens if we're not satisfied with a clean before Sunday morning?
Ask about their complaint resolution process. How quickly will they respond if something is missed the night before a service?

7. Do you provide a written scope of work?
Any provider unwilling to put the details in writing is a red flag. Your committee needs a documented task list to hold them accountable.

8. What are your contract terms and cancellation policy?
Understand the commitment length, auto-renewal terms, and what it costs to leave early.

9. Do you have specific experience with faith facilities?
Sanctuaries, pews, nurseries, and fellowship halls all have unique requirements. Church-specific experience is genuinely useful.

10. How do you schedule around our worship calendar?
Ask how they handle special services, weddings, funerals, and holiday schedule changes without disrupting the regular routine.
