Cleaning Standards for Church Nurseries and Childcare Spaces

Cleaning a nursery is a fundamentally different task from cleaning a sanctuary. The stakes and standards are different.

Cleaning a nursery or children's classroom is a fundamentally different task from cleaning the rest of a church building. Children touch surfaces constantly and bring hands to faces, eyes, and mouths far more than adults do. The result is faster transmission of common illness through a congregation's youngest members — and their families — if these spaces aren't handled with the right standards.

Why children's spaces are higher risk

Shared toys, changing surfaces, and close physical contact between children create conditions for illness to spread quickly through a nursery or Sunday school classroom. A cleaning approach that treats these spaces the same as an adult meeting room misses the point.

Product safety first

A key differentiator for nursery and classroom cleaning is product selection. Ask your provider whether they use low-fragrance, child-safe cleaning products in these specific spaces, and whether their disinfectants require extended ventilation time before children re-enter — important for spaces used again the very next morning.

Key cleaning areas in nursery and children's spaces

  • Toys and play equipment: Hard toys should be regularly washed and disinfected; soft toys laundered on a set schedule
  • Changing areas: Sanitized after every use during service hours, and thoroughly cleaned as part of the regular schedule
  • Classroom surfaces: Tables, chairs, door handles, and shared supplies disinfected weekly at minimum
  • Floors: Vacuumed and, where applicable, mopped with child-safe products
  • High-touch surfaces: Check-in counters, cubby areas, and door handles wiped down regularly

Communication with parents

Churches with active nursery and childcare programs should be able to describe their cleaning approach to parents on request. If your cleaning provider can supply a documented schedule for nursery-specific tasks, that's worth sharing with your children's ministry team — it builds confidence with parents dropping off young children.

Questions to ask a provider about nursery cleaning

  • Do you use different products in nursery and classroom spaces than in the rest of the building?
  • Is your staff background-checked, given the sensitivity of children's spaces?
  • Can you clean around our Sunday morning and midweek children's program schedule without disrupting drop-off and pickup?
