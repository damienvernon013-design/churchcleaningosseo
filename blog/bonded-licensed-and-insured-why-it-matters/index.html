Bonded, Licensed & Insured: Why It Matters for Church Cleaners

These words sound reassuring, but many facilities committees don't know what each one actually protects against.

You've probably seen these words in a church cleaning company's materials: “bonded, licensed, and insured.” They sound reassuring, but many facilities committees don't know what each one actually means — or why the distinctions matter when things go wrong in a building that also houses a congregation's most vulnerable members during nursery hours.

What “licensed” means

A business license means the company is legally registered to operate. It's the baseline requirement for any legitimate business. Ask for the company's business registration and verify it if your committee wants to be thorough.

What “insured” means — and why it's critical

General liability insurance
This covers property damage caused by the cleaning crew — a broken window, a knocked-over sound board, chemical damage to a hardwood platform — and third-party injuries resulting from their work. Standard coverage is typically in the range of $1M–$2M per occurrence.

Workers' compensation insurance
If a cleaning employee is injured on church property, workers' comp covers their medical expenses and lost wages. Without it, the church could be exposed to those costs even though the injured person isn't a church employee. This is the coverage most often missing from underpriced cleaning companies.

What “bonded” means

A surety bond (also called a janitorial bond) is financial protection against employee theft. If a cleaning employee steals from the building — not uncommon in unsupervised evening access — and the company is bonded, the church can file a claim to recover the loss.

How to verify

Always ask for a certificate of insurance (COI) before signing any contract. The COI lists the insurer, policy numbers, coverage types, and expiration dates. Request to be named as an additional insured on the policy — this means your church is notified if coverage lapses.
