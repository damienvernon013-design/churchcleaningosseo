How to Get the Best Church Cleaning Quote Without Overpaying

A little preparation before the walkthrough makes the difference between a fair quote and a guess.

Getting a church cleaning quote is easy. Getting a good one takes a bit of preparation. Facilities committees that consistently land fair prices for reliable service share a few common habits — and they all happen before the cleaning company ever walks through the doors.

Know your square footage

This sounds obvious, but many churches give rough estimates. Cleaners price by the space — the more accurate your numbers for the sanctuary, fellowship hall, classrooms, and restrooms, the more accurate and fair your quote will be. Walk the building and know which spaces are included versus excluded (storage rooms, mechanical rooms, unused wings).

Define your scope before the walkthrough

Before inviting providers in for a quote, write down exactly what you need: which spaces, which tasks, how often, and what the non-negotiables are — a spotless narthex before Sunday service, for example. This prevents providers from quoting a stripped-down service and prevents your committee from accepting something that won't actually keep the building ready for worship.

Get at least three quotes

One quote gives you a number. Three quotes give you a market. The spread between the lowest and highest bids for identical work can be significant. Middle-range quotes are usually the sweet spot — the cheapest bids often involve underqualified staff or hidden fees, while the most expensive aren't necessarily better.

Ask what's not included

Always ask providers to tell you what falls outside their standard quote. This surfaces hidden costs before your committee signs anything, not after.

Request references from similar congregations

A cleaning company that excels at office buildings may not be the right fit for a sanctuary with pews and a chancel carpet. Ask for references from other churches or faith facilities of similar size.

Negotiate strategically

  • Offer a longer contract in exchange for a locked-in rate
  • Ask if quarterly or annual payment reduces the monthly cost
  • Compare quotes within the same week — quotes are often valid for a limited window
