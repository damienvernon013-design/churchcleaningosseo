10 Areas Most Churches Miss During Cleaning

Even buildings that look clean often harbor neglected spots that accumulate grime and bacteria over time.

Even church buildings that look clean at a glance often harbor neglected spots that accumulate dust, grime, and bacteria over time. If your cleaning routine isn't addressing these areas, you're getting a partial clean — and your congregation is experiencing the difference, even if they can't name it.

The 10 most commonly missed areas

1. Pew crevices and hymnal racks
Dust, dropped bulletins, and crumbs from children's snacks accumulate in pew joints and hymnal racks. These are rarely addressed in a quick weekly pass.

2. Light switches and door handles
High-touch surfaces that almost never get wiped down in a standard cleaning routine, despite being touched by nearly everyone who enters the building.

3. Nursery toys and changing surfaces
Toys and changing tables see constant contact but are often cleaned less thoroughly than hard classroom surfaces. These deserve dedicated attention, not an afterthought.

4. Tops of bookshelves and high storage in classrooms
Out of sight, out of mind — until a volunteer reaches up for supplies and comes away with a dusty hand. High dusting requires deliberate effort and the right tools.

5. Fellowship hall kitchen appliance handles
Refrigerator handles, coffee maker buttons, and oven controls see constant use during community meals but are often wiped only on the visible exterior surfaces.

6. Air vents and return grilles
Dusty vents blow allergens directly into the sanctuary's air during worship. These should be wiped monthly and deep-cleaned quarterly.

7. Chair bases and stackable chair legs
Rolling and stackable chairs used in fellowship halls and classrooms pick up debris from every floor surface they're moved across.

8. Sound booth and AV equipment surfaces
Touched by the same one or two volunteers every week, sound booth and AV surfaces are frequently skipped by cleaning crews unfamiliar with the space.

9. Entryway mats and narthex tracks
Mats trap dirt and moisture but rarely get pulled up and cleaned underneath. In areas with wet winters, this becomes a real slip hazard if ignored.

10. Under and behind pews and furniture
Dust, dropped items, and debris accumulate under fixed seating and behind furniture. Without periodic furniture-moving during deeper cleans, these spots go unaddressed for months.
