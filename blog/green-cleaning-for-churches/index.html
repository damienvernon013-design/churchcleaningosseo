Green Cleaning for Churches: Why Eco-Friendly Products Are Worth Considering

Green cleaning is increasingly the smarter choice for buildings full of children, elderly members, and shared air.

The cleaning industry uses a significant volume of chemical products across every facility type. Many of these products contain compounds that contribute to indoor air irritation and can trigger allergies or asthma — a genuine concern in a sanctuary full of shared air for an hour or more at a time, or a nursery with young children present daily.

What makes a cleaning product “green”

  • Third-party certified for reduced human health and environmental impact
  • Biodegradable formulations that break down without harmful residues
  • Low-fragrance or fragrance-free options, particularly relevant for members with sensitivities

Health benefits for your congregation

Many conventional cleaning products release compounds that contribute to headaches, eye irritation, and respiratory discomfort that some members may notice, particularly those with asthma or chemical sensitivities. Green products reduce that exposure meaningfully, especially in enclosed spaces like a sanctuary during a full-length service or a nursery used for hours every week.

A stewardship consideration

For congregations that think about environmental stewardship as part of their mission, cleaning product choices are a small but concrete way that value shows up in the building itself. It's worth raising with a facilities committee alongside more visible stewardship decisions.

The cost question

Green cleaning products have historically carried a price premium, but the gap has narrowed significantly. When evaluating cleaning proposals, ask providers to specify which products are used in which spaces, and whether green-certified options are available at a comparable price point.
